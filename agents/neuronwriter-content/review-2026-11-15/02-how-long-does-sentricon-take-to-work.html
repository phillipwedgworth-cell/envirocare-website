Why is termite baiting not instant?

Subterranean termites forage through the soil all the time, looking for cellulose. Sentricon termite bait stations are installed in the ground around the perimeter of the house, spaced to sit in that termite foraging path. Worker termites that find a station feed on the bait and carry it back to the colony, where it is passed along to other termites. The active ingredient works slowly on purpose: it has to move through the colony before it takes effect, so the workers keep feeding and keep sharing instead of dying on the spot. The goal is the colony itself, not just the termites that happen to cross a treated line; Sentricon is labeled for colony elimination of subterranean termites.

With Sentricon Always Active™ technology, every bait station contains bait from the day the stations are installed. There is no waiting for termites to find an empty monitor first.

Separate installation, evidence of feeding, assessment of activity and continuing maintenance. These are milestones to document, not deadlines promised for every home. Ask the specialist how the inspection findings determine follow-up for this property.

What affects how fast it works

Colony size and distance

A small colony foraging near the foundation responds faster than a large, established colony feeding from across the yard. Formosan termites, which are established along Alabama's Gulf Coast, build much larger colonies than the Eastern subterranean termites we find across central and north Alabama, and larger colonies take longer.

Soil temperature and season

Termites forage less in cold soil. Alabama winters are mild, so activity slows in December and January rather than stopping, and feeding picks up in spring and through the warm months. The stations keep working year-round either way.

How many termite colonies are on the property

A lot near a creek, a wooded edge or a drainage area can have more than one colony foraging under it. Each one has to find the Sentricon bait, and the bait stations around the perimeter are spaced so that they can.

Conditions around the house

Wood touching soil, mulch piled against siding, leaking spigots and wet crawl spaces all make a house more attractive to termites. Fixing those helps the program and is worth doing regardless. If there is a previous termite problem or active termite infestation in the structure, the technician will tell you whether anything beyond the bait stations is needed.

What you will, and will not, see

Signs of termite activity to keep watching for

While the stations work, keep an eye out for the same signs of termite activity as always: mud tubes on the foundation, a swarm of winged termites in spring, shed wings on a windowsill, or wood that sounds hollow. Any of those means a call to us, not a can of spray.

What the technician sees

Most homeowners never see the system working, and that is normal. What the technician looks for on each service visit is bait consumption in the stations and, over time, a drop in foraging activity. The outcome you are protecting is no termite activity in the structure, and the service visits are how that is checked.

If you see termites or mud tubes inside the house while the stations are in place, call us. Do not spray them yourself: disturbing active termites can break up the feeding that the bait depends on. Our guide to identifying termites helps you tell a swarm of termites from flying ants first.

Monitoring and maintenance: what the service visits involve

The Sentricon system is not install-and-forget; continuous monitoring is the point. On a schedule, a pest control technician checks every station, looks for signs of feeding and for foraging between stations, replaces bait as needed, and notes conditions around the house that invite termites. The green caps you see flush with the lawn are those stations; our post on green caps in the yard explains what they are.

Sentricon bait stations vs. liquid barrier treatments

Liquid treatments and termite bait systems are both established approaches to termite control and they work differently. A liquid termiticide is applied to the soil around the structure and acts quickly on termites that cross it; a bait system takes longer and works through the colony's own feeding. Which fits depends on the house, its construction and how much activity is already present.
