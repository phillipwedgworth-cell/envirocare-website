Where do mosquitoes breed on a shoreline property?

Still water in shade. The inside of a covered slip where the water does not move. A dock box, a bailing bucket, a life-jacket bin with an inch of rain in the bottom. Kayaks and canoes stored right-side up. The saucers under the deck planters. The low corner of the yard where the lake backs up after a rain. And the cove itself, where a shaded, sheltered inlet holds water that barely moves. You cannot do anything about the cove. You can do something about everything else, and it is worth doing weekly from March through October.

Reference: Empty or cover water-holding containers and maintain screens. CDC: Mosquito control at home.

Where they rest — and why that is the target

Adult mosquitoes do not hover over water during the day. They rest on the underside of leaves, in ivy and shrubs, under the deck, along the shaded foundation, in the boathouse rafters, under the dock. A barrier treatment goes on those surfaces, so mosquitoes that land there over the following weeks do not get up again. On a lake lot the treated zone is the band between the house and the shoreline, the boathouse, and the shaded side yards — which is where you sit, and where the bites happen.

What treatment can and cannot do on the water

A monthly barrier treatment knocks the population in the treated zone down substantially and keeps it down; that is what makes a porch usable in July. It does not get rid of every mosquito on a lake, and anyone who tells you otherwise is selling something. Mosquitoes fly in from the cove and the neighbor's untreated lot; a treated lot has far fewer, not none. Treatment near water is done with products labeled for that use, applied per label directions, and the technician stays off the water's edge itself. The setting is exactly why we schedule the eight treatments across the whole season instead of a couple of summer sprays.

Things that help that nobody does

  • Store kayaks, canoes, and the dinghy upside down.
  • Drill a drain hole in the bottom of the dock box and the life-jacket bin.
  • Run a fan on the dock and the porch. Mosquitoes are weak fliers; a fan on medium clears a seating area.
  • Cut the ivy and the low shrubs back from the path between the house and the dock.
  • Dump the boat cover after every rain.

Mosquito larvae and breeding sites around the dock

Female mosquitoes lay eggs on or near standing water, and the larvae develop in that water before adult mosquitoes emerge. That is why reducing breeding sites matters as much as treating resting areas. Walk the property after rainfall and check the places water sits: boat covers, dock boxes, clogged gutters on the boathouse, low spots in the landscape and drainage that backs up near the lake. Where water cannot be drained or covered, such as an ornamental pond, ask whether a larval control product labeled for that site is appropriate. Larvicide is not applied to the lake itself as part of a residential service.

Do mosquito traps or mosquito misting systems help?

Mosquito traps and automatic mosquito misting systems are sold for lake homes. Traps catch some mosquitoes but do not by themselves keep mosquito populations low across a yard. Misting systems spray on a timer whether or not mosquitoes are present, and the nozzles near a shoreline need careful placement. Neither replaces removing breeding sites and treating the shaded areas where mosquitoes rest. If you are weighing one, ask what it targets and how it is maintained.

Protecting your family on the dock

Treatment reduces mosquito pressure; it does not stop every mosquito bite. The CDC recommends EPA-registered insect repellents, including products with DEET or picaridin, used according to the label. Citronella candles have limited effect on their own. Mosquito-borne illnesses such as West Nile virus occur in Alabama, so repellent and long sleeves at dusk still make sense on the water.

Frequently asked questions about lake house mosquito control

Is mosquito spraying near the lake toxic to fish?

Some insecticides are toxic to fish and other aquatic life, which is why product labels restrict how close to water they can be applied. Our technicians use products labeled for the site and keep treatments off the water's edge.

How often should a lake house be treated?

Treatment frequency varies with the property, but regular treatments throughout the season do more than one-time spraying before a holiday weekend. Our program spaces treatments across March through October so protection is in place when you are using the dock.
