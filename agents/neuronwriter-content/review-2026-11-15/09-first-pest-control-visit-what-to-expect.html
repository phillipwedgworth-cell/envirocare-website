How to prepare for your first pest control visit

You do not have to clean the house. It helps to have the areas where you have seen pests accessible — under the kitchen sink, the pantry floor, the garage corners — and to have pets secured. If you know where you have seen activity, tell the technician; a photo of the insect saves time. Have the gate unlocked if the backyard is fenced. If nobody is home, exterior service can still happen; interior work waits for a time you are there.

The thorough inspection: the walk around the outside

This is where the value is. The technician walks the full perimeter looking at the foundation line, the weep holes, where the AC line and hose bib and cable enter the wall, every door threshold and garage seal, crawlspace vents, mulch depth against the slab, gutters and downspouts, and anything touching the siding. In Alabama that walk also catches things you did not call about: a mud tube on a pier, a wasp nest starting in a soffit, a gap a mouse will use in October. You should get a plain description of what was found and what it means.

What gets treated, and where

The exterior treatment goes on the surfaces insects actually cross: the foundation band, around doors and windows, the garage threshold, eaves and soffits, and the shaded cover where they rest. Granular material goes in beds and along the drip line where appropriate. Eaves get treatment that discourages wasps from building; an active nest can be treated as an add-on. For ants, non-repellent material and bait go at the nest sites and trails rather than a broadcast spray. Everything used is EPA-registered and applied per label directions, and the technician will tell you the re-entry timing for treated areas.

Inside the home: treating where the pest problem lives

Interior treatment on a first visit is targeted: bait in the cracks where roaches live, treatment at the plumbing penetrations under sinks, the garage, and the attic or crawlspace access if the inspection found reason. It is not a baseboard spray of every room — that is the old way, and it is not how pests are reached. If the problem is German cockroaches or fleas, the technician will explain that a follow-up visit is part of the plan, because the egg stage does not respond to the first treatment.

How long it takes, and the follow-up visit

Visit length and follow-up depend on the property, pest and inspection findings; ask the technician what to expect for your planned service. More visible activity after treatment does not by itself establish success or failure. Record sightings and ask the technician about persistent or unexpected activity rather than applying another product. If covered pests show up between scheduled visits, call us about the re-service included in your plan. What happens during your first pest control visit: the signs of pests a technician looks for

A thorough inspection of your home comes before any treatment plan. The technician looks for signs of pest activity: droppings along cabinet bases and in the garage, shed cockroach skins, ant trails along a counter or window, spider webs in corners and eaves, and gnaw marks or nesting material that point to rodents. They also note entry points such as gaps under doors, openings where pipes and wires penetrate the wall, and torn screens, plus conducive conditions like plumbing leaks, damp crawl spaces and mulch piled against the foundation. Common household pests in Alabama, including ants, cockroaches, spiders, silverfish and rats and mice, each leave different signs, so tell the technician what you have seen and where.

Things to expect from your first professional pest control visit

  1. A conversation first. The technician asks what pest issues you have seen, where and when, and whether you have tried DIY pest control products, since some can interfere with baits.
  2. An inspection inside and around your home. Expect a look for signs of activity: rodent droppings, roach skins, ant trails, nest material and gaps around the foundation.
  3. A treatment plan explained before work starts. You should know which areas will be treated, what preparation is needed and any re-entry directions.
  4. The initial pest treatment. Exterior first, then targeted interior work where the inspection found reason.
  5. A service report and next steps. What was found, what was applied, what the homeowner can fix, and when the next visit is scheduled.

Knowing what to expect makes the first appointment shorter and more useful. When you are ready to schedule your first visit, call the office that serves your area.

What should your pest control plan include?

After the first inspection and treatment, you should know what pest problem was found, which areas were treated, what the technician recommends you fix (a leaking faucet, a gap under the garage door) and when the next service is. Ongoing pest control is built around that plan: routine visits keep a protective treatment around the home, and re-service handles covered pests that show up between visits. Ask the technician to point out anything they want you to watch for so you can report it on the follow-up visit.

Frequently asked questions about a first pest control visit

Should I clean my house before the exterminator comes?

No deep cleaning is needed. Clear the areas the technician needs to reach, such as under sinks and along garage walls, put away food and dishes on counters, and keep pet bowls and pet food out of treatment areas.

Do I need to leave the house during treatment?

For routine exterior service, usually not. For interior work, follow the re-entry and preparation instructions the technician gives for the products used, including any directions for people and pets.

Do I have to wash everything after pest control?

Not routinely. Follow the instructions for your specific treatment. Avoid mopping or wiping treated cracks and baseboards right away, since that can remove bait or residual material, and call us with any question about a treated area.
