What should regular service address?

A visit should consider the activity reported, accessible evidence and conditions that support pests. Inspection, entry-point recommendations and changes to food, water or shelter can matter alongside treatment. Any pesticide application must fit the product label and the findings.

The EPA's prevention guidance recommends addressing those conditions and learning about the pest before selecting controls. It does not prescribe one treatment interval for every Alabama property.

Does a product always last sixty or ninety days?

Do not assume a fixed period from a service calendar alone. Product directions, the application site and conditions at the property guide treatment decisions. A quarterly schedule does not by itself prove a month without protection, and an every-other-month schedule does not mean another application is always needed.

Ask what the provider checks during each visit and what would trigger follow-up between visits. A useful plan explains both the scheduled work and how new activity is assessed.

When might a different schedule be needed?

An active infestation may require follow-up based on the identified pest and treatment method. Commercial facilities may have access, monitoring and reporting requirements that change the scope. Those decisions should be documented for the property rather than inferred from a statewide rule.

Can a limited problem be handled as a separate job?

Ask whether the proposed work is a one-time service, a service with follow-up or an ongoing plan. A visible nest or indoor infestation still needs identification and an assessment of access and scope. Confirm what completion means and what happens if activity continues.

How often should a house be treated for pest control in Alabama?

For most Alabama homes, routine general pest control every two to three months is a common schedule, and we service on an every-other-month (bi-monthly) basis. The reason is the climate: Alabama's warm, humid weather keeps ants, cockroaches, spiders and other insects active for much of the year, and mild winters mean pest pressure rarely stops entirely. A regular schedule lets the technician renew the protective treatment around the home, inspect for new entry points and moisture problems, and catch activity early.

Some pests follow a different calendar. Mosquito control is seasonal, March through October. Termite protection is separate from general pest control: subterranean termites are a structural threat, and homeowners in Birmingham, Huntsville and across Alabama typically keep an annual termite inspection under a termite agreement. Rodent problems, a German cockroach infestation or fleas may need follow-up visits closer together until the activity is under control.

This approach follows integrated pest management: inspect, correct the conditions pests use (food, water, shelter and gaps), and treat where the evidence supports it, rather than spraying on a fixed calendar alone.
