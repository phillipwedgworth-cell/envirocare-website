Is Sentricon Worth It? An Honest Look at the Yearly Cost

People do not usually ask whether Sentricon® works. They ask whether it is worth paying for every single year, forever, when the termites never showed up in the first place. That is a fair question and it deserves a straighter answer than most of our industry gives it. The Wedgworth family has been doing termite control in Alabama since 1958, and EnviroCare is fourth generation now, so we have watched this question get dodged for a long time.

What You Are Actually Paying For

A Sentricon installation is two costs, not one. There is the install, where stations are placed every 10 to 15 feet around your foundation, and then there is the annual renewal that keeps the system monitored and keeps your coverage in force. The renewal is the part people question, so let us be plain about what it buys.

Somebody physically comes and checks. A technician opens the stations, looks for termite activity, replaces spent bait, and notes what is happening around your foundation. That is labor on a schedule, not a subscription fee for nothing.

The coverage stays live. Sentricon installations we service carry up to $1,000,000 in termite damage repair coverage provided by EnviroCare, subject to the terms of the agreement. That coverage is ours, not the manufacturer's. It lapses if the renewal lapses.

You get a paper trail. Alabama regulates termite work, and a documented, continuously serviced system is what a buyer's attorney wants to see when you sell a house.

We do not publish a flat termite number, because there is not one. Linear footage, foundation type, and what the inspection turns up all move it. Termite pricing is set after a free inspection.

Who Sentricon Is Worth It For

If you plan to stay in the house. The renewal is inexpensive relative to a single termite repair bill. Alabama has subterranean termites in essentially every county, and the damage is slow and hidden. By the time you see it in a baseboard, it has been happening for a while.

If you are going to sell in the next few years. A continuously serviced termite system with records behind it removes a negotiation point at closing. A lapsed one creates one.

If your house is hard to treat by other means. Stamped concrete, a finished basement floor, a pool deck, or anything else you would rather not drill through makes a bait system the practical choice.

If you have had termites before. Conducive conditions rarely change on their own. The house that got termites once is the house that gets them again.

Who It Is Probably Not Worth It For

We would rather tell you this up front than sell you something you will resent paying for.

You are selling this year and the buyer will start fresh. Sometimes the right move is a current wood destroying organism inspection letter, not a new termite system you will hand over in four months.

The structure is not worth protecting. A detached shed or an outbuilding you would tear down before repairing does not need a monitored termite system around it.

You will not keep the renewal current. A system nobody services is a row of plastic caps in your yard. If the renewal is not realistic for your budget, say so and we will talk about what else makes sense. A lapsed termite system protects nothing.

The Question Nobody Asks, But Should

What happens in year six?

Liquid soil treatments degrade. That is not marketing, it is chemistry. The termiticide breaks down in the soil over a period of years, and the barrier thins out whether or not anyone tells you. A monitored bait system does not have that clock, because the bait is replaced as it is consumed and somebody is looking at your foundation on a schedule.

That is the real argument for the Sentricon renewal. You are not paying every year because termites are chewing on your house every year. You are paying so that the year they do show up is not also the year you discover your termite protection quietly expired.

What We Would Tell You At Your Kitchen Table

We have installed both kinds of termite protection in Alabama for a long time, and we have seen how each one ages. When someone asks whether the Sentricon renewal is worth it, the answer depends on how long you are staying and what the house is worth to you, not on which system we would rather sell.
