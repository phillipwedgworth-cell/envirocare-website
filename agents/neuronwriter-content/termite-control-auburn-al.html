Signs of a termite problem every homeowner should know

Eastern subterranean termites live in the soil and build mud tubes to reach wood. Watch for:

  • Pencil-width mud tubes on the foundation, piers or slab edge.
  • Swarmers or discarded wings near windows and doors, most often in spring.
  • Wood that sounds hollow, blistered paint or structural damage behind trim.
  • Soft spots in flooring near a bathroom or kitchen.

These signs can look like other insect or moisture problems. Take a photo, leave the area undisturbed and call rather than spraying it with a household chemical; EPA guidance on identifying and controlling termites recommends the same: confirm the evidence, fix moisture problems and get a professional treatment plan. Findings in accessible areas do not rule out concealed damage.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is termite protection worth it?

Subterranean termites are active across Alabama, and damage is often hidden until repairs are expensive. Ongoing protection with monitoring, plus a written agreement that spells out repair provisions, gives you a plan and someone to call.

How can I protect my home and prevent termites?

Keep mulch and soil a few inches below siding, fix leaks and gutter overflow, store firewood away from the house and keep wood from touching the ground. Each measure reduces conducive conditions, and the most effective technique still pairs prevention with professional protection from a pest control company that monitors the home.
