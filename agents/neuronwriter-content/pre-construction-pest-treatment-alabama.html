What Pre-Construction Termite Treatment Is

Pre-construction termite treatment, often called a pretreat, is termite protection installed during the build rather than after occupancy. Subterranean termites reach a structure from the soil beneath and around it, so the soil is where the termite treatment belongs. Alabama's warm climate, long season, moisture, and clay soils keep subterranean termite colonies foraging for most of the year, which is why a pretreat is standard practice on new construction here rather than an upgrade.

A new building is not protected simply because it is new. Untreated soil under a fresh slab is an open path, and framing lumber stacked on bare ground during construction is an invitation on its own. Handling termite control at the construction stage also produces the paperwork the owner will need later, which is what keeps a closing on schedule.

Two Approaches for New Construction

Soil Termiticide Pretreatment

A soil pretreat applies a labeled termiticide to the graded soil and to critical areas before the slab is poured. Application is done in stages that match the build: horizontal treatment of the pad and footing trenches, then treatment at plumbing penetrations, expansion joints, and the perimeter after backfill. Rate and coverage follow the product label, and the work is documented. It is a proven approach and it is what many lenders and builders expect to see on a new construction file.

Sentricon Always Active Bait System Installation

The second approach is a bait system. EnviroCare is a Sentricon Certified Specialist, and we install Sentricon Always Active in-ground bait stations around the completed footprint. Each station holds active termite bait from the day it goes in the ground and is monitored on a schedule. Foraging termites feed on the termite bait and carry it back to the colony, which addresses the nest rather than only the soil line at the foundation. A bait system requires no slab drilling and no chemical trenching, and it converts directly into an ongoing termite protection plan for the eventual owner.

On many projects the right answer is a soil pretreat during construction plus a Sentricon installation at completion, so the structure carries both the construction-stage treatment and an ongoing monitored bait system. We will tell you plainly which combination fits the lot, the foundation type, and the buyer's expectations.

Frequently Asked Questions About Pre-Construction Termite Treatment

When in the build should the pretreat happen?

For a soil treatment, after final grade and before the slab is poured, with follow-up treatment at penetrations and the perimeter after backfill. Bait system installation happens once the structure is complete and the ground around it is at final grade. We coordinate both with the superintendent so nothing gets poured over untreated soil.

How long does pre-construction termite treatment last?

A soil termiticide barrier degrades over time and its useful life depends on soil type, moisture, drainage, and disturbance from later landscaping or utility work. A monitored bait system does not expire on a fixed schedule; it remains effective as long as the service plan is active and the stations are inspected and replenished. That difference is a large part of why many builders now pair the two.

Are there Alabama requirements for new construction termite treatment?

Termite work in Alabama must be performed by a licensed pest control company, applying registered products according to the label, and the wood infestation report issued at closing is a regulated document. Individual lenders, loan programs, and local building officials may have their own expectations for what appears in the file, so we document thoroughly.

What does pre-construction termite treatment cost in Alabama?

It is priced after a free on-site inspection, based on square footage, foundation type, site conditions, and which approach the project calls for. There is no charge for the inspection or the quote, and we will price a multi-lot schedule for builders running several homes at once.

Do you work with builders and general contractors directly?

Yes. Most of our pre-construction work is contracted with the builder or GC rather than the eventual homeowner, and we handle the documentation the buyer and lender need at closing.

What if a termite infestation shows up after the home is occupied?

A structure on an active termite protection plan is monitored, and any termite activity found is addressed under that plan, which includes up to $1,000,000 in damage repair coverage, subject to the terms of the agreement.
